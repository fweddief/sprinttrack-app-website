How Do I Get Faster in the 100m?

Science-backed ways to improve your 100m time: acceleration, max velocity, strength, and smart periodization for sprinters.

Getting faster in the 100m is not about running more random sprints. The race is won in the first 30 meters (acceleration) and maintained through max-velocity mechanics from 40–60m. Train both qualities on separate days so each session has a clear purpose.

Block starts and drive-phase work teach horizontal force. Fly-in sprints, wicket runs, and assisted overspeed (where appropriate) teach upright top-end speed. In the weight room, prioritize explosive leg strength—squats, Olympic derivatives, and plyometrics that match your training phase.

Progressive overload still matters: log sessions, rate effort (RPE), and adjust volume before you chase new PRs every week. A structured block—general prep → specific prep → taper → competition—beats grinding the same workout year-round.

Apps like SprintTrack help by periodizing around your meets and prescribing target paces from your PRs, so each rep has a number to hit instead of guessing.
